What the forestry side knows

The numbers above come from landscape irrigation, which has spent decades measuring how water lands. The judgment below comes from wildland structure protection, which has spent decades watching what happens when it lands in the wrong place.

The objective is damp fuel, not a wet house

The purpose of structure-protection sprinklers is to wet the receptive fuels next to the structure and raise humidity in the air around it — not to hose down the building. Stated plainly in the doctrine: it does not require a lot of water.

Structure Protection Guide · App. I

Houses shed falling water, not driven water

A roof and wall assembly is designed for rain arriving from the sky. An impact head drives water sideways with force, past siding and into the wall cavity. The damage may show immediately in a log wall — or surface months later as black mold behind sheetrock.

Structure Protection Guide · App. I

A settable arc is the preferred head

Two kits are common in the national cache: a full-circle-only impact head, and a part-circle head on a tripod. The part-circle is preferred because it allows close-in placement without throwing water onto the structure. Set the arc outward and the wall stays dry.

Structure Protection Guide · App. I

Close-in, angled, three to four feet off the corner

With a full-circle head, the field fix is placement rather than pattern: stake it angled and leaning toward the value at risk, roughly three to four feet from the corner, so the pattern dampens ground cover without saturating siding.

Structure Protection Guide · App. I

Overlap the patterns

Forestry says to choose placements that let adjacent patterns overlap. Irrigation says the same thing with a number: head-to-head spacing — each head throwing to the base of the next, which is spacing equal to the radius. Ninety percent of radius is better in wind.

Irrigation Association / Rain Bird · head-to-head

Trunk and laterals, not one long hose

Avoid feeding heads from a single straight run of ¾-inch hose; performance falls off down the line. Run a larger trunk line with laterals branching every hundred feet or so, and pressure at the last head stays closer to the pressure at the first.

Structure Protection Guide · pump & water handling

Fifty psi is the design point

Cache sprinklers are built to run at 50 psi. Below it the radius collapses and the spacing you planned no longer overlaps; above it the droplets shatter and drift. Pressure is the variable that quietly invalidates a layout.

Structure Protection Guide · pump & water handling

And the reason this system exists

Standard advice given to homeowners before an evacuation includes: do not leave sprinklers running — it affects water pressure. That is the coordination problem stated by the fire service itself. Everyone protecting their own house at once is how a neighborhood loses its supply. Scheduling turns is the whole point.
