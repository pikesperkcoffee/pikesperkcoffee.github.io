About the Editor

Andrew Morton edits The Morton from Palmer Lake, Colorado — a workshop, a roastery, and a long stretch of keeping things running where conditions were not favorable.

The Shop

He operates Morton Millworks — a workshop running an apprenticeship on the bench — under the parent Morkat Coffee LLC. Pikes Perk Coffee Roasters, purchased in recent years, now operates under the same parent.

Roasting and CNC millwork are different trades that reward the same habit: you learn the machine well enough to know what it is telling you before it tells you loudly. A roast curve and a tool path are both records of a process you have to read while it is happening.

The apprenticeship runs on the same principle. Real machines at every rung, and nothing signed off that has not been done.

A decade of range operations is a decade of the same question asked in different weather: what happens if this goes wrong, who gets hurt, and what did we have in place before it started. Live fire does not grade you on intent. The fire service asked the same question with less warning and worse visibility.

The tech-control background sits underneath both. A message that arrives across a bad channel is not the message that was sent, and the discipline is knowing how to tell how much of it survived. That habit shows up in most of what gets written here.

Systems that only work under good conditions were never working. They were being carried by conditions.

The Written Work

His written work moves across wildland fire policy, industrial history, symbolic inheritance, and the working relationship between operators and the tools they use.

Recent essays are filed in the Register on the front page. Longer standing work is under Of Record. New tiles land as new work does.
